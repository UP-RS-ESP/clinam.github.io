The *Climate and GNSS in Namibia* (CLINAM) experiment is an advanced network of continuous GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) and meteorological stations situated within Namibia. Launched in March 2026, the network currently operates 4 active scientific stations strategically located in northern and southern Namibia.

CLINAM is led by the University of Potsdam in collaboration with [Sophienhof](https://sophienhof-lodge.com/de/), the [Ongava Research Center](https://orc.eco/), and the [Gondwana Kalahari Anib](https://gondwana-collection.com/).

**Core Scientific Objectives**
- Atmospheric Water Vapor Sensing: Processing direct GNSS signals to quantify integrated water vapor (IWV) and reconstruct 3D atmospheric moisture transport dynamics.
- GNSS Reflectometry (GNSS-R) & Hydrology: Leveraging reflected GNSS signals to perform high-resolution monitoring of surface soil moisture and near-surface hydrological processes.
- InSAR Atmospheric Corrections: Utilizing high-density water-vapor observations to eliminate tropospheric noise and refine InSAR surface displacement measurements across the Olkaria Geothermal Complex.
- Hydrologic Deformation: Tracking sub-centimeter ground motion to better characterize subsurface hydrological dynamics.
- In-Situ Meteorological Observations: Recording continuous, high-frequency weather variables—including air temperature, barometric pressure, and relative humidity—to analyze localized microclimates and storm dynamics.
